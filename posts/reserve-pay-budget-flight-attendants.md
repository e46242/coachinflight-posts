For a lot of crew (flight attendants on reserve, ramp and ground crew picking up shifts), budgeting can feel like trying to navigate through turbulence. One month you're flying big hours; the next you're sitting reserve on guarantee.

But here's the trick: when your income fluctuates, **your budget should flex** too.

## 🧳 The Challenge of Variable Income

If you're on reserve, living on guarantee, or picking up trips and extra shifts when you can, your income isn't always predictable. Some months you land a great line or a stack of pickups. Other months, you're relying on just the basics.

That's where a traditional fixed-budget system can fail. It's hard to stick to when your paycheck feels more like a rollercoaster.

## ✈️ The 80/20 Rule for Variable Income

Enter the **80/20 budget**. This method is all about treating your variable income like a flexible asset while giving you a **consistent plan for living expenses**.

Here's the breakdown:

- **80%** of your income goes toward essentials (fixed living costs like rent, utilities, groceries, etc.).
- **20%** is for **savings** and **extra goals** (savings, debt payments, travel, etc.).

**Why it works:** If you have a big month, you save more. If you have a smaller month, you still know your basics are covered.

## 📊 The Power of Buckets

To really make this system work, break things down into **buckets** (or categories).

You'll have three main ones:

1. **Essential Bucket** (rent, utilities, etc.)
2. **Discretionary Bucket** (fun stuff, food, entertainment, etc.)
3. **Savings Bucket** (retirement, emergency fund, other goals)

**How it works:** When you have a bigger-than-usual month, fill your savings bucket extra. If you're in a leaner month (say, a month on reserve guarantee), your savings bucket might be smaller, but you're still putting something in.

## 🧠 Pro Tip: Automate When Possible

If you're not already, **automate** as much as possible. Have a fixed amount of each paycheck go straight to savings, like clockwork. This helps you save **consistently**, even when your income feels unpredictable.

## 🚀 The Flexibility of This System

This budgeting method works no matter how big or small your paycheck is.

Even in months where your trips are light, you can rest easy knowing your essentials are covered and you're still building your financial future, little by little.

## 🛬 Final Thoughts: Your Budget Should Adapt to You

If your income swings, a fixed budget will keep fighting you.

It's all about flexibility. The key is having a system that works with your income, **not against it**.

With a solid plan, you can weather both the stormy months and the smooth skies.
