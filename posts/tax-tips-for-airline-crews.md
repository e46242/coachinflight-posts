Let's get something out of the way:

Taxes can be extra confusing for airline workers.

Not because we're reckless. Not because we're bad with money. But because the tax system wasn't designed for people who:

- Work across states
- Get per diem
- Have variable income
- Pick up trips or shifts inconsistently

The good news? Once you understand the basics, taxes stop feeling mysterious and start feeling manageable.

> **Note:** Tax rules change and depend on your situation. Verify anything here with a tax professional before acting on it.

## ✈️ Tip #1: Per Diem Is Not "Free Money"

Per diem feels great because:

- It often isn't taxed
- It pads your paycheck
- It doesn't feel like income

But here's the catch: per diem can make your **taxable income look lower than your real income**.

That's not bad. It's just important to understand.

Why it matters:

- It affects Roth vs. Traditional decisions
- It affects tax bracket planning
- It affects how "low" your income looks on paper

This is one reason many crew look closely at **Roth accounts** earlier in their careers. (Last week's post, [The 401(k) Match](https://coachinflight.com/blog/401k-match-dont-reject-a-pay-raise), covers the first step before that.)

## 🧠 Tip #2: Withholding ≠ What You Owe

This one trips people up every year.

Your paycheck withholding is just an **estimate**, not the final answer.

That's why:

- Some people get big refunds
- Some people owe unexpectedly

Refunds aren't a bonus. They're just your money coming back late.

The goal isn't a big refund. The goal is **accuracy**.

## ✈️ Tip #3: State Taxes Can Get Weird (Fast)

If you:

- Live in one state
- Are based in another
- Fly through many

You already know this can get confusing. States handle this differently, and some don't tax income at all.

This is where a tax professional who understands **aviation pay structures** can be worth their weight in gold. Not fancy. Just familiar with your reality.

## 🧾 Tip #4: Deductions Are Tighter Than They Used to Be

A lot of older advice may no longer apply.

Things many crew *used to* deduct (uniforms, luggage, meals, crash pads) are generally **not deductible for W-2 employees** under current rules. Confirm with a tax pro for your year and situation.

That doesn't mean taxes are unfair. It just means the strategy changed.

The focus now is on:

- Account choice
- Timing
- Planning ahead

## 📊 Tip #5: Taxes Are a Planning Tool, Not a Surprise

The biggest shift you can make:

Stop thinking of taxes as something that happens *to* you. Start thinking of them as something you **plan around**.

Good tax planning:

- Happens during the year
- Informs where you invest
- Guides Roth vs. Traditional decisions

April should be confirmation, not panic.

## 🛬 Final Thoughts: Awareness Saves Money

You don't need to memorize tax law.

You just need to understand:

- How your pay is structured
- Which levers you actually control
- When to ask for help
