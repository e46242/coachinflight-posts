Most people get stuck obsessing over *what* to invest in.

The bigger question is *where*.

Same investments, different accounts, very different results, because each account handles taxes differently. Once you know which bucket comes first and when to move to the next, the rest of personal finance gets a whole lot simpler.

Here's the flight plan.

## 1️⃣ The 401(k) Match: Step One, Always

If your employer offers a 401(k) match, get the full match before anything else. It's part of your pay. (More on this in the next post.)

## 2️⃣ Roth IRA: Pay a Known Tax Now

For many airline workers, the next bucket worth a close look is a **Roth IRA**.

The basic difference (no jargon):

**Traditional IRA**

- You get a tax break *now*
- Your money grows tax-deferred
- You pay taxes when you withdraw in retirement

**Roth IRA**

- You pay taxes *now*
- Your money grows tax-free
- You withdraw tax-free in retirement

Same investments. Different tax timing.

The real question isn't "Which account is better?" It's: **"Will my tax rate be higher later, or is it higher now?"**

**Why crew income can look lower than it really is:** Between per diem, retirement contributions, and irregular schedules, our **taxable income** often looks smaller on paper than our lifestyle suggests. For many crew, especially earlier in their careers, that can mean a lower bracket today, which is where Roth tends to shine.

Roth IRAs give you:

- Tax-free growth
- Tax-free withdrawals
- Control over your investments
- Flexibility later in life

Think of it like paying for your seat at today's known price instead of guessing what it'll cost later.

**"But I want the tax break now..."** Totally fair. A Traditional IRA can still make sense if:

- You're in a high tax bracket *today*
- You need immediate tax relief
- You expect significantly lower income in retirement

Roth vs. Traditional really does depend on your situation. If you're unsure, run it by a tax professional.

## 3️⃣ HSA: The Triple-Threat Account

If you qualify for a Health Savings Account, this thing is a unicorn.

It's:

- Tax-free going in
- Tax-free growing
- Tax-free coming out (for medical expenses)

Used correctly, an HSA can quietly become one of the most powerful retirement tools you have. Don't ignore it.

## 4️⃣ Taxable Brokerage: The Overflow Tank

Once your tax-advantaged accounts are in motion, extra money can go into a regular brokerage account.

No tax breaks upfront, but:

- Full flexibility
- No age rules
- Great for bridging the gap to early retirement

Think of this as your long-haul fuel reserve.

## 🧠 Common Mistakes Airline Crew Make

- ❌ Jumping straight into a brokerage account and skipping tax-advantaged accounts
- ❌ Obsessing over *what* to invest in instead of *where*
- ❌ Thinking "I don't make enough yet" (spoiler: you don't need to)

You don't need to be perfect. You just need to be **intentional**.

## 🛬 Final Thoughts: Start Simple, Stay Consistent

You don't need to do all of this at once.

Start with:

- The match
- One account
- One habit

That's enough to get airborne.

You can't control future tax laws. But you *can* control which bucket your money grows in and how much flexibility you have later.
